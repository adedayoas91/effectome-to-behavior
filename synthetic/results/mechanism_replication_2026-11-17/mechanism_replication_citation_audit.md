## Project application and claim boundary

For each method, the project forms one score per independent global-null process seed: the maximum estimated probability-reduction score across the fixed candidate units and both supported directions. Scores, not oracle truth or later test outcomes, determine the calibration threshold. With `n` complete calibration seeds and nominal `alpha`, the order-statistic rank is

`r = ceil((n + 1) * (1 - alpha))`.

The rank must be at most `n`; otherwise these data cannot yield a finite calibrated cutoff at that level. Let `q` be the `r`th ordered calibration maximum. The adopted operational threshold is

`tau = max(0.10, q)`.

The 0.10 probability-unit floor preserves the existing development cutoff and is a project design choice. Nomination requires a score **strictly greater** than `tau`. Since `tau >= q`, the floor only reduces the nomination event relative to the rank cutoff. Exact ties do not create extra nominations.

The application uses the elementary exchangeable-rank argument: for a fresh null score generated by the same complete procedure, `P(M_new > q) <= (n + 1 - r)/(n + 1) <= alpha`, with conservative treatment of ties. Here the score and controlled event are defined locally; this is not the tutorial's original prediction-set target. The calibration argument requires exchangeability of the complete score-generating procedure, including data generation, training-owned model/support fitting, candidate/direction aggregation, rollout calculation and estimator randomness. It does not establish causal identification or eliminate model misspecification.

The pinned conventional CPU estimator has no public API that controls its threaded Numba permutation draws. Requested seeds therefore do not establish byte-identical conventional rediscovery or demonstrably independent permutation streams. The fixed procedure retains the first completed graphs, native evidence and provenance; restarts reuse them, without favorable rerun selection. Exchangeability of those complete stochastic scores is an assumption of applying the rank result. Thread/resource limits manage execution, not RNG validity.

## Nineteen calibration seeds and sixty untouched null-test seeds

For `n=19`, `alpha=0.05` gives `r=19`: the calibration cutoff is the observed maximum before applying the conservative floor. The resulting protection is **marginal** over calibration and future null-score randomness. It is not a 95% confidence statement that this particular realized threshold has null nomination probability at most 5%. It also supplies no candidate-level FDR, simultaneous guarantee over all methods, or strong error control in scenarios containing real drivers.

The separate 60-seed null test evaluates the frozen realized rule. One null seed contributes one event: any nominated candidate/group in either direction. Its one-sided exact 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bound uses `BetaQuantile(0.95; k+1, n-k)` for `k<n` and 1 for `k=n`. With zero events among 60 test seeds the bound is approximately 0.04870. Coverage concerns a fixed method/rule and independent test seeds; it is not simultaneous over methods. Calibration seeds must not be added to this denominator. A failed or incomplete test must not trigger threshold retuning on those test outcomes or selective replacement seeds.

These are complementary statements: marginal rank calibration does not ensure that a finite independent null audit passes its confidence-bound criterion. The 0.10 floor may be more conservative, but its actual benefit and power cost require the prespecified test, not an assumed result.
